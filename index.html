01 / The situation

The problem is bigger than a late release.

When leadership cannot tell what is working, who controls the accounts, or whether the next release is safe, the company has lost the ability to make technical decisions.

A new agency can add people. It cannot create truth from the outside. Recovery begins by building an evidence-backed record of the product, the delivery operation, and the risks the business is carrying.

01

Progress exists only in status meetings.

Leadership cannot verify what shipped, what remains, or why the plan moved again.

02

The vendor controls critical access.

Repositories, cloud accounts, signing identities, domains, and deployment knowledge sit outside company control.

03

Every release depends on heroics.

Testing is inconsistent, rollback is uncertain, and a routine change can become a business incident.

04

No one can explain the real run-rate.

Agency fees, cloud spend, tooling, and rework accumulate without a decision-quality view of cost.

03 / The difference

A recovery engagement, not another dependency.

I work on the client side of the table. The objective is to improve the company’s ability to see, decide, and deliver without making my continued presence a hidden requirement.

Ground truth

Evidence before prescription

Recommendations follow repository, infrastructure, account, delivery, and cost evidence. The audit does not begin with a predetermined diagnosis.

Ownership

Client control from the start

Repositories, accounts, infrastructure, payment methods, and operating knowledge remain under company control.

Governance

Automation with hard boundaries

Appropriate routine work can be automated. Production, money, identity, security, legal, and destructive decisions stay with people.

Continuity

A useful exit is part of the work

Decision records, runbooks, onboarding guidance, access records, and continuity documentation are maintained as operating assets.

05 / The boundary

Automation handles appropriate work. People retain consequential judgment.

Tools may investigate, draft, test, review, report, and propose. They do not approve their own production access or make unilateral decisions that can materially harm the company.

06 / Fit

Built for a real operating problem.

Strong fit

  • A real software product with revenue, funding, or material operating dependence
  • Five-figure monthly delivery cost or comparable business exposure
  • Missed launches, vendor opacity, unstable releases, or unclear ownership
  • A decision-maker willing to provide the access required for an independent audit

Not the right fit

  • Idea-stage product development
  • A predetermined conclusion about a vendor or team
  • Deadline, savings, or security guarantees
  • Production automation without testing, rollback, and human approval gates
